> **The short answer**
> You don't need a new playbook. AI answers are built from pages that search engines can already crawl, read and trust, so good SEO is still the entry ticket. What's new is how you write the page (lead with the answer), what you put on it (proof only you can give) and where you're talked about (places off your own site).

> **Three takeaways**
> - **SEO, AEO and GEO are one shift, not three jobs.** Links, then answers, then AI-written answers. Each stage builds on the one before.
> - **The goal is to be the source the answer cites.** Ranking still matters. Being named and described correctly matters now too.
> - **Skip anyone selling GEO as a secret trick.** Ask what it does that good SEO doesn't.

SEO. AEO. GEO. Three acronyms, and it's easy to assume you've fallen behind on all of them.

You haven't. Let's sort out what changed and what didn't.

## What's the difference between SEO, AEO and GEO?

**They're three stages of one shift in how buyers get answers.** Loren and I cover this in the second edition of our book. Think of it as a ladder:

1. **Links (SEO).** You earn a place in a list of links. The buyer sees ten results and clicks one. The work is keywords, useful pages, speed, links from other sites and local listings.
2. **Answers (AEO).** Search engines began answering the question on the results page itself, with featured snippets, "People also ask" boxes and voice assistants. The buyer may never click.
3. **Generated answers (GEO).** AI now writes a new answer from several sources and cites a few. That's Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode, plus ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Microsoft Copilot. Your goal is to be one of the sources.
4. **Agents (next).** AI agents will browse, compare and act for the buyer. They'll need the same clear, current facts, plus a site they can use.

In the book, SEO means the whole job. AEO and GEO name the answer-focused part of it, which we call AI search visibility.

## Are AI answers really taking clicks?

**Yes, and the numbers are worth knowing.** A few from the research we cite:

- In a 2025 Pew Research study of 900 U.S. adults, people clicked a regular search result on 8% of visits when an AI summary appeared. When it didn't, they clicked on 15%.
- A Washington University in St. Louis study of 55,393 Google searches in March and April 2026 found AI Overviews on 13.7% of all searches and on about 65% of searches phrased as questions.
- Seer Interactive found that on informational searches, brands cited in an AI Overview earn 120% more organic clicks per impression than brands left out. That's still about 38% fewer clicks than when no AI Overview appears.

Being cited softens the loss. That's the whole point of working on this.

## Do I need special files or markup for AI?

**No.** Google's own 2026 guide to its generative AI features says optimizing for them is still SEO. There are no extra requirements, no special files or markup, and no need to chop pages into small chunks.

One caution. That guide covers Google's AI features only. It says nothing about ChatGPT, Perplexity or Copilot. Treat it as the floor, not the whole picture.

## What should I add to my SEO?

**Seven things, in this order.**

1. **Lead with the answer.** Put the answer to the question in the first lines of the page. Then explain.
2. **Add proof.** Name your sources, quote real people and use your own numbers. AI favors pages it can verify. In the GEO research paper, adding citations, quotations and statistics raised a source's visibility by up to 40% in the authors' tests. That test used a simulated AI engine, not Google or ChatGPT, so treat it as a hint, not a promise.
3. **Publish what only you can.** Your projects, photos, data and opinions are the difference. Anyone can generate a generic page.
4. **Earn mentions off your site.** Reviews, directories, partners, local press and helpful answers in the forums your buyers use all feed AI answers.
5. **Make your facts match everywhere.** Name, services, prices, hours and service area should agree on your site, your Google Business Profile and your listings. Keep them current.
6. **Open the door to every engine.** Let the right crawlers in, verify your site in Bing as well as Google, and keep your Business Profile and product feeds in order.
7. **Measure citations, not just rankings.** Track how often AI mentions you, which pages it cites and what it says about you.

## What should I skip?

**Four things.**

- **llms.txt and special AI files.** Google says it doesn't need them and ignores them.
- **Mass-producing pages.** A separate page for every variation of a question can break Google's spam policy on scaled content. More pages don't make a better site.
- **Keyword stuffing.** It didn't help in the GEO research, and it never read well.
- **Anyone selling a secret.** If a vendor says GEO is a separate trick, ask what it does that good SEO doesn't. I'd like to hear the answer, too.

## How do I check what AI says about my business?

**Ask it the questions your buyers ask.** Pick five to ten real buyer questions. Ask ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Google's AI features each one. Write down whether you're mentioned, whether the facts are right and which pages get cited. Do it again every month.

Neither Google Analytics nor Search Console tells you what AI says about you. They'll show visits from AI assistants and impressions in Google's AI features. The "what does it say" part you check by hand.

Then fix what you find: wrong hours, an old service, a missing answer. That's usually a page edit, not a project.

## Your next three actions

1. Write down five buyer questions and ask each AI assistant. Record what comes back.
2. Pick your most important page. Rewrite the first two sentences so they answer the question directly.
3. Check that your name, services and hours match on your site and your Google Business Profile.
