Most teams don't need a bigger AI strategy. They need a 90-day AI plan that starts with one workflow and ends with a way of working.

I've watched plenty of teams try to transform everything at once. They buy tools, run a kickoff, and three months later a few people use AI and most don't. Nothing is written down. Nothing is shared. That's Level 2 or 3 on the AI fluency ladder, and it's where most teams stall.

The goal is Level 4: AI is a written step in your workflows, the prompts are saved and shared, and a person approves what AI produces. Here's how to get a team moving toward it in 90 days.

## Why don't AI tools alone get a team using AI?

**Because the value comes from changing how the work gets done, not from having the tool.** Giving everyone an AI tool is the easy part, and it's where most businesses stop.

## Where should a team start with AI?

**With one deliverable your team makes every month.** The monthly report, the blog post, the ad set, the client recap. Then:

1. Time it the way you do it today.
2. Write one CRAFT prompt for it: Context, Role, Ask, Format, Tighten.
3. Save it where the team can find it.
4. Name the person who checks and approves every draft.
5. Time it again, and show the team what changed.

## What happens in month 1?

**You run one workflow for real and agree on ground rules before anyone builds anything.**

![The 90-day plan: month 1 one workflow and ground rules, month 2 three workflows and a prompt library, month 3 a written policy and a quarterly review.](/images/blog/get-marketing-team-using-ai-3.png)
*That's Level 4 for one team.*

**Weeks 1 and 2: set up.** Agree on your AI ground rules first. We use five:

- People approve everything public. No exceptions.
- Verify every fact. AI is confident, not always right.
- Protect your data. Know what never goes in a prompt.
- Keep your voice. If it doesn't sound like you, rewrite it.
- Add what AI can't: your stories, judgment and experience.

Start your AI usage policy now, too. Name an owner for each priority, and record your starting numbers so you'll know what changed.

**Weeks 3 and 4: quick wins.** Run your first workflow for real. Time it, approve it with the named person and share the result with the team. Pick two or three other quick wins from the top of your list, like a broken form or tracking that doesn't work.

## What happens in month 2?

**You build three workflows and start a shared prompt library with the prompts your team uses most.** Here's the important part: every person on the team builds one. People support what they help create.

Hold a 30-minute weekly check-in: what's done, what's next, what's stuck. And protect the time. If the calendar doesn't change, nothing changes. For every new task you add, take an old one away.

If you have more candidate workflows than time, rank them. ## What happens in month 3?

**You write it down and review it.** Finish your AI usage policy and agree on it as a team. Write a training plan. Take a clear look at roles: who drafts, who checks, who decides and who owns the system.

Then hold your first quarterly review. Compare your numbers to where you started. What's working? What isn't? What do we change? Have everyone retake the AI fluency self-assessment, so you can see the climb.

That's Level 4 for one team. It isn't a software project. It's a management project.

## Why do AI rollouts stall, and how do you make one stick?

**They stall when nothing is written down, shared or owned.** Five habits fix most of it.

**1. No more than three priorities at a time.** Be realistic about your team's hours. They already have full-time jobs, and every task in the plan lands on top of what they already do.

**2. A name on every approval.** "The team" doesn't approve anything. A person does. Write the name next to each workflow.

**3. Time before and after.** The before-and-after numbers are your proof. They're what earns you the next 90 days.

**4. Celebrate the quick wins out loud.** Tell the team and your leadership what changed and what it did. Early proof builds support for the harder work that follows.

**5. Put the review on the calendar today.** A review date turns a plan into a commitment. Invite everyone who owns part of the plan.

## FAQ

### How long does it take to get a team using AI every day?

This plan takes 90 days: one workflow and ground rules in month 1, three workflows and a shared prompt library in month 2, and a written policy and quarterly review in month 3.

### What AI ground rules should a marketing team have?

We use five. People approve everything public. Verify every fact. Protect your data. Keep your voice. Add what AI can't: your stories, judgment and experience.

### How do I get team members who resist AI to use it?

Show them their own before-and-after times, and have every person build one workflow. People support what they help create.

### Who should approve AI-generated marketing work?

A named person, not "the team." Write the name next to each workflow so everyone knows who checks and approves every draft.

### Does a small team need an AI usage policy?

Yes. Start it in month 1 and finish it as a team in month 3. It covers who approves what, what data never goes in a prompt and which tools are approved.
