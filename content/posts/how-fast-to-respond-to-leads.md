> **The short answer**
> Reply to every new lead within one hour during business hours, and within 15 minutes if you can. Send an automatic reply right away that says what happens next and when. Same day is the minimum. Many businesses take half a day or longer to answer, so a business that replies within the hour stands out.

> **Three takeaways**
> - **Aim for one hour.** Within 15 minutes is excellent. Same day is the minimum.
> - **Name an owner.** If no one owns the inbox, no one answers it.
> - **Sort hand-raisers from researchers.** Call the ones who ask fast. Nurture the rest.

When we researched the Austin pool market for my brother Dan's company, [Wild West Custom Pools](https://www.wildwestcustompools.com), we read the negative reviews of the big builders. The same complaint came up again and again: you wait three, four, five days for somebody to call you back.

That's a speed to lead problem, and it isn't just pool builders. Most businesses are slow to answer the people who ask to talk.

## How long do most businesses take to respond to a lead?

**Often half a day or more.** In one 2026 test, the average email reply took nearly 12 hours.

![Workato 2026: average email reply nearly 12 hours, phone call-back about 14.5 hours, nearly one in five never replied by email.](/images/blog/how-fast-to-respond-to-leads-1.png)
*If your competitors take half a day, the hour is your opening.*

In that study, published in March 2026, Workato submitted demo requests to 114 B2B companies and timed the replies. Only one company sent a personal email within five minutes. The average email reply took nearly 12 hours, and phone call-backs averaged about 14 and a half hours. Nearly one in five companies never replied by email at all (Workato, 2026).

Workato sells automation software, and these were B2B companies, so read it with that in mind. The lesson still applies to local businesses.

Buyers' expectations are moving the other way. In EZ Texting's 2026 report, a survey of 959 U.S. consumers, nearly 70% said they expect a business to reply to a text within an hour. That's a texting vendor's survey, so treat it with care, but the direction is clear.

If your competitors take half a day, the hour is your opening.

## Can fast response be part of your brand?

**Yes.** A promise like "call the owner directly" turns speed into a reason to choose you, as long as you keep it every time.

Here's what I like about Dan's position. "We only build 12 pools a year" isn't just about scarcity. It's about access. One of the search ads in his playbook says, "Call the Owner Directly. Always." When a buyer reaches out, they talk to Dan, not a call center. No queue, no ticket.

That's the speed-to-lead lesson turned into a promise. And a promise like that only works if you keep it every time.

## What lead response time standards should you set?

**An automatic reply right away, a real reply within an hour during business hours, and never later than the same day.** Here's what Loren and I recommend in the book for anyone who fills out a form, requests a quote or calls:

- **Right away:** an automatic thank-you that says what happens next and when.
- **Within 15 minutes:** excellent. This is where you win buyers who are contacting several businesses at once.
- **Within one hour, during business hours:** the goal for every business.
- **Same day:** the minimum. A reply tomorrow tells the buyer they're not a priority.
- **After hours:** an AI assistant can answer, ask two qualifying questions and book a call. It can't quote prices or promise dates. Tell people they're talking to an AI.

![Lead response standards: auto-reply right away, 15 minutes excellent, one hour the goal, same day the minimum.](/images/blog/how-fast-to-respond-to-leads-2.png)
*After hours, an AI assistant can greet, ask two questions and book a call.*

Fast beats perfect every time. A short, real reply in 20 minutes beats a polished one tomorrow.

## How can I respond to leads faster without hiring more staff?

**Test your own response time, name one person who owns it, fix your auto-reply, sort hot leads from curious ones and set rules for any after-hours AI.** Five things to do this week:

**1. Time your own form.** Fill out your contact form on a weekday morning, the way a buyer would. Then do it again on a Saturday. Write down how long it takes for a person to reply. Do the same with a phone call. Most owners are surprised, and not in a good way.

![Five things to do this week: time your form, name an owner, fix the auto-reply, sort hot leads, set AI rules.](/images/blog/how-fast-to-respond-to-leads-3.png)
*Most owners are surprised by step one, and not in a good way.*

**2. Write the standard down and name who owns it.** "We answer every inquiry within one business hour" means nothing until a person is responsible for it. If no one owns the inbox, no one answers it. Name a backup for days off.

**3. Fix the automatic reply.** Most auto-replies say "Thanks, we'll be in touch." Say what happens next and when: "[Name] will call you within the hour during business hours. If it's after 5 PM, expect a call by 9 AM." AI can draft three versions in your voice in a minute. You pick one and make it sound like you.

**4. Sort the hot leads from the curious ones.** Not every lead is the same. Someone who asks for a quote is raising a hand. Someone who downloads a guide is doing research. Add a simple question to your forms, like "When are you hoping to start?" The answer sorts your leads better than any score. Call the hand-raisers fast. Nurture the researchers with useful email instead of a sales call five minutes later.

**5. Set rules before you add an AI assistant.** If you want something answering after hours, write its script first and approve it under your AI usage policy. It can greet, ask two questions and book a time. It never quotes prices, promises dates or pretends to be a person.

## How do you measure speed to lead?

**Track your average time to first reply every month.** Whatever standard you set, measure it. How many inquiries got a same-day answer. How many never got one. Put it on the same scorecard as your leads and sales. What gets measured gets answered.

## FAQ

### What is speed to lead?

Speed to lead is the time between a buyer reaching out, by form, quote request or call, and the first real reply from a person at your business.

### Do I really need to respond within five minutes?

Five minutes is great if you can do it. For most small businesses, the goal is within one hour during business hours, with 15 minutes as excellent and same day as the minimum.

### What should an automatic reply to a new lead say?

What happens next and when. For example: "[Name] will call you within the hour during business hours. If it's after 5 PM, expect a call by 9 AM."

### Can AI answer my leads after hours?

Yes, with rules. An AI assistant can greet the buyer, ask two qualifying questions and book a call. It should never quote prices, promise dates or pretend to be a person.

### Who should be responsible for responding to leads?

One named person, with a named backup for days off. If no one owns the inbox, no one answers it.
