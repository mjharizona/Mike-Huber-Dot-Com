My new talk opens with a show of hands: How many of you worry that AI could take your job? I raise my own hand first.

If you lead a marketing team, assume most of your people would raise theirs. Some will tell you. Many won't. The question of AI and marketing jobs is already in the room whether you bring it up or not.

Here's how Loren Ankarlo and I think about answering it.

## Will AI replace marketing jobs?

**AI will change marketing roles.** Pretending it won't is how you lose your team's trust. I just rebuilt my book, my website and a whole set of tools around it with AI. Work that used to wait in line for a writer, an editor, a designer and a developer got done by two people with AI drafting and checking.

![Microsoft and LinkedIn Work Trend Index: 75% of knowledge workers use generative AI; only 26% say leadership is clearly aligned on AI.](/images/blog/talk-to-team-about-ai-job-fears-1.png)
*The question isn't whether your team uses AI. It's whether anyone is leading it.*

That has consequences for how marketing teams are staffed.

And AI is already in their work. In Microsoft and LinkedIn's 2024 Work Trend Index, 75% of knowledge workers said they use generative AI at work, and 78% of those users said they bring their own tools rather than waiting for their employer. In the 2026 edition, which surveyed 20,000 AI users in ten countries, only 26% said their leadership was clearly aligned on AI, and 65% feared falling behind.

So the question isn't whether your team uses AI. It's whether anyone is leading it.

## Why do employees resist AI?

**Because change feels like loss.** Loren has spent his career on the people side of change, and he puts it plainly: "Change equals loss. People resist loss. Demonstrate WIIFM equals gain." What's in it for me?

People worry about their jobs, their skills and their status. When that fear goes unspoken, it goes underground. It shows up as slow-walking, quiet pushback and your best people updating their résumés.

It also shows up in what they say. "AI content is garbage" is often pride in craft and a fear of being devalued. "We don't have time to learn this" is often overload and a fear of looking incompetent. And sometimes you just get silence, and then nothing changes.

> “Change equals loss. People resist loss. Demonstrate WIIFM equals gain.”
>
> — Loren Ankarlo

## What should a manager say and do about AI job fears?

**Tell the truth, write it down, fund training and involve the team.** Here are five things to do, starting this month.

![Five things to do: tell the truth early, write the FAQ, fund training, put the skeptic in charge of quality, let the team redesign workflows.](/images/blog/talk-to-team-about-ai-job-fears-2.png)
*People resist what's done to them and support what they help build.*

**1. Tell the truth about roles, early.** Don't promise "no one's job will change." Roles will change. Say how, and what you'll do to prepare people for the new version of their job. When you haven't decided something, say that, and say when you will. A confident promise you can't keep does more damage than an honest "we haven't decided yet, and here's when we will." Send the announcement at least two weeks before the work starts, so people hear it from you and not the rumor mill.

**2. Write the FAQ before they ask.** Draft an announcement and an FAQ that answers the ten toughest questions, including "Will AI replace my job?" AI can help you draft it. Use a CRAFT prompt and tell it not to guess or promise anything you haven't said. Here's how [the CRAFT prompt framework](https://marketingfieldguide.ai/blog/write-better-ai-prompts-small-business?utm_source=mikehuber.com&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=q1-2027&utm_content=talk-to-team-about-ai-job-fears) works. Then have HR and someone with a change-management lens review it, and say it in your own words.

**3. Put a training line next to the software line.** The Content Marketing Institute's 2027 research found that team skills and capabilities were the second biggest factor in effective B2B programs, at 43%, while marketing technology came in eighth, at 15%. Yet only 9% of B2B marketers named human resources as a top investment area for 2027, versus 30% for AI tools (CMI, 2026). If you'll spend on the tool, spend on the training. Every dollar for AI software should come with time for the people who will use it.

**4. Put the skeptic in charge of quality.** The person who says AI content is garbage usually cares most about the work. Give them the job of setting the quality standard for AI output. They'll catch what others miss, and they'll have a real role in the new way of working.

**5. Let the team redesign its own workflows.** People resist what's done to them and support what they help build. The people doing the work know best where AI helps and where it doesn't. Ask them which tasks AI could take on, then let them write the new steps.

## How will marketing roles change with AI?

**People move from making every piece to directing and checking the work.** Here's the part I want every writer, designer and developer to hear. Everything I built with AI got better every time a skilled person touched it.

![How roles shift: writers from first drafts to final say, designers from making assets to directing them, developers from every line to owning the system.](/images/blog/talk-to-team-about-ai-job-fears-3.png)
*The people who get stronger are the ones who learn to direct AI first.*

When we built the website for my brother's pool company, [Wild West Custom Pools](https://www.wildwestcustompools.com), AI's first draft said he personally designs every pool. He doesn't. He works with a designer. AI wrote it confidently, and it was wrong. Someone who knew the business had to catch it.

AI writes fast and sounds sure. It needs someone who knows what good looks like. Writers move from first drafts to final say. Designers move from making every asset to directing them. Developers move from writing every line to owning the system. The people who get stronger are the ones who learn to direct AI first.

So here's my answer to the question in the room. I can't promise anyone their job is safe, and nobody can. What I've seen in my own work is that the people who build AI into the work, check it and teach the rest of the team are in the strongest position.

## FAQ

### Should I promise my team that AI won't replace their jobs?

No. Roles will change, and a promise you can't keep costs you their trust. Say how roles will change, what you'll do to prepare people, and when you'll decide what you haven't decided yet.

### When should I tell my team about AI changes?

At least two weeks before the work starts, so people hear it from you and not the rumor mill.

### What should an AI announcement FAQ cover?

The ten toughest questions your team will ask, including "Will AI replace my job?" Have HR and someone with a change-management lens review it before you share it.

### How do I get a skeptical team member on board with AI?

Put them in charge of quality. The person who says AI content is garbage usually cares most about the work, so let them set the standard for AI output.
