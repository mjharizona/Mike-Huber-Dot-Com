Last year I told my brother Dan to build only 12 pools a year. Then a friend told me to take my own advice. This is how I'm focusing my own business in 2027, and why I think the same idea might help yours.

## Why does limiting what you do work?

**Because a clear limit tells the right buyers what they'll get, and turns the wrong ones away.** I saw it first with my brother's business.

![What "We only build 12 pools a year" tells a buyer: they won't be one of 40 jobs, the owner is on-site, they get his phone number.](/images/blog/focus-business-doing-too-much-1.png)
*The number turns most buyers away. That's why the right ones call.*

Dan owns [Wild West Custom Pools](https://www.wildwestcustompools.com) in the Austin area. When we repositioned his company, the big idea wasn't a new website. It was a number: "We only build 12 pools a year."

That number does a lot of work. It tells a buyer they won't be one of 40 jobs. It tells them the owner will be on-site from excavation to first swim, and that they'll have his phone number. It turns most buyers away, and that's exactly why the right ones call.

I believed every word of that. Then I spent the next six weeks doing the opposite.

## How do you end up doing too much?

**One reasonable yes at a time.** I've written about this before, so here's the short version.

I started by updating my CV with AI. Then my website. Then Dan's business. Then the book Loren Ankarlo and I wrote in 2023. Then the interactive PDF, a website for the book, online tools, a field guide for every skill, facilitator's guides and training slides.

Every one of those was a yes, and every one made sense. At the top of the stairs, I had one more idea: with all this material, we could do some consulting too. Sell the book, sell subscriptions to the website and do consulting. Three businesses.

Then I had lunch with Arnie, an old friend and one of the trusted advisors on the project. He asked me how I was going to make money, and what my main focus was. When I told him, he said it was way too much. Then he said, "Just because you can doesn't mean you should."

## What Arnie told me

I asked him what he meant. Here's what he said, as close as I can tell it:

When you describe working with your brother, who only builds 12 pools a year, I think you should take the same advice you gave him. Only build 12 marketing programs a year, with 12 different businesses. Reposition them, or help them fine-tune what their marketing should be. Just 12.

Twelve.

And I said, a little sheepishly: well, maybe 10 and 2.

## How do you focus your own business?

**Pick your number, write down who you're not for and stop something for every new thing you start.** You don't need to run a consulting practice for this to apply. A marketing team that does twelve things well beats one that does forty things halfway. Five lessons:

**1. Pick your number.** How many clients, projects, products or channels can you give your full attention to? Write the number down. Then look at how many you're carrying today.

![Five steps to focus: pick your number, write who you're not for, write a done-when sentence, make a stop list, review quarterly.](/images/blog/focus-business-doing-too-much-3.png)
*Focus drifts. The quarterly review is what keeps it.*

**2. Write down who you're not for.** Dan isn't for the buyer who needs the cheapest pool. I'm not for the business that wants overnight results. A clear no makes it easier for the right people to say yes.

**3. Write a "done when" sentence for the year.** "This year is a success when ____." Everything you're tempted to add has to argue its way in against that sentence.

**4. Make a stop list.** For every new thing you take on, take something away. If you can't name what you'll stop doing, you don't have room for the new thing yet.

**5. Review it every quarter.** Focus drifts. Put a quarterly review on the calendar and ask the same questions each time: what's working, what isn't, and what do we change? ## FAQ

### How many clients should a consultant take on at once?

As many as you can give your full attention to. For me in 2027, that's ten marketing programs and two consulting clients. Your number will be different, so write it down and compare it with what you're carrying today.

### How do I decide what to stop doing?

Make a stop list. For every new thing you take on, take something away. If you can't name what you'll stop, you don't have room for the new thing yet.

### Won't turning customers away hurt my business?

It turns the wrong ones away. Dan's "We only build 12 pools a year" turns most buyers away, and that's exactly why the right ones call.

### How often should I review my focus?

Every quarter. Focus drifts, so put the review on the calendar and ask the same questions each time: what's working, what isn't, and what do we change?
