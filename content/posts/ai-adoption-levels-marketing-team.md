Almost every marketing team I talk to uses AI. Very few have built it into how they work. That gap is what the AI fluency ladder is for.

The numbers say the same thing. In a Goldman Sachs 10,000 Small Businesses Voices survey of 1,256 small business owners (January 27 to February 4, 2026), 76% said they use AI. Only 14% are fully integrating it into core operations.

Three in four use it. About one in seven have built it in. Let's talk about the space in between.

## What are the four levels of AI adoption?

**Not started, experimenting, using regularly and integrated.** Loren and I use this simple ladder in the book:

- **Level 1: Not started.**
- **Level 2: Experimenting.** You and a few people try it now and then.
- **Level 3: Using AI regularly.** People use it every week, but it depends on who remembers.
- **Level 4: Integrated.** AI is a written step in your workflows, the prompts are saved and shared, and a person approves what it produces.

If your team uses AI but hasn't built it in, you're at Level 2 or 3. That's where most teams start, and there's nothing wrong with it. The trouble is staying there.

## What does an integrated (Level 4) team look like?

**The process gets the results, not the one person who remembers.** On the second edition of our book, the work grew faster than two people could track. Four weeks in, Loren and I had about 60 pieces in flight. What kept it moving wasn't a better tool. It was a written process.

Every evening I left notes on the draft. Overnight, AI applied the clear changes, drafted the bigger ones for my approval and ran the checks. Every morning I got a one-page brief of what changed and what needed me. Same steps, same rules, every night. Nobody had to remember to do it.

That's the difference between Level 3 and Level 4. At Level 3, a good person gets good results. At Level 4, the process gets good results, and the person decides.

## Should we invest in AI tools or AI training first?

**Training. Here's what most teams get backward.** They buy tools and skip people.

![Content Marketing Institute 2027 research: team skills ranked second at 43%, technology eighth at 15%, yet 30% invest in AI tools and 9% in people.](/images/blog/ai-adoption-levels-marketing-team-2.png)
*Most teams buy tools and skip people. The research says that's backward.*

The Content Marketing Institute's 2027 research (861 B2B marketers, surveyed June to August 2026) asked marketers with effective programs what made the difference. Team skills and capabilities came in second, at 43%. Marketing technology came in eighth of 12, at 15%. Yet only 9% named human resources as a top investment area for 2027, while 30% named AI-powered marketing tools (CMI, 2026).

Getting from Level 3 to Level 4 isn't a better tool. It's process, people and a plan.

## How do you move your team up one level?

**Place the team, rate skills with proof, write one workflow down, protect learning time and share what works.** Here are five ways to climb one rung.

![Five ways to climb one rung: place the team, rate skills with proof, write one workflow down, protect learning time, run a show-and-tell.](/images/blog/ai-adoption-levels-marketing-team-3.png)
*One written workflow is your first real step toward Level 4.*

**1. Place your team where it is today.** Not where you wish you were. A team of strong individuals can still be at Level 2 if no workflow is written down. The ladder measures how AI is built into your team's work, not how talented anyone is.

**2. Rate skills with proof.** We use six AI skills: briefing, evaluating, iterating, delegating, protecting and systematizing. Rate yourself 1 to 5 on each. Here's the rule that keeps it honest: you only get a 4 if you can point to a real task where you did it on your own in the last month. Write that task down. The free [skills assessment](https://marketingfieldguide.ai/skills-assessment?utm_source=mikehuber.com&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=q1-2027&utm_content=ai-adoption-levels-marketing-team) gives you your average and level, and sends your lowest scores to a skills-gap plan.

**3. Write one workflow down.** Pick a task someone does every week and complains about. Write the steps: what AI drafts, who checks it, who approves it. Save the prompt where the team can find it. That one written workflow is your first real step toward Level 4.

**4. Protect learning time.** In 2025, employees averaged 16.7 hours of formal learning for the year, about 1.4 hours a month (ATD, 2026). That isn't enough to build AI skill. Put two to four hours a month on every calendar, and defend it. Then add an hour or two a week of practice on real work. That's where the skill gets built.

**5. Run a monthly show-and-tell.** Each person shares one AI workflow that saved time and one that failed. The winners go into a shared prompt library the whole team can use. People learn faster from each other than from a course, and it makes it safe to admit what didn't work.

## Why does AI adoption stall?

**Because the new way is slower before it's faster.** Level 2 is where most people feel the dip, and some people will want to go back. That's normal. The dip ends. The cost of staying out doesn't.

Make it small to get through it: one task, one tool, 30 days. Small wins give people proof that it works.

## FAQ

### What is the AI fluency ladder?

It's a four-level scale Loren Ankarlo and I use in our book: not started, experimenting, using regularly and integrated. It measures how AI is built into your team's work, not how talented anyone is.

### What is the difference between using AI and integrating AI?

Using AI depends on who remembers to open it. Integrating AI means it's a written step in a workflow, with saved prompts and a person who approves the output.

### How much training time does a team need to build AI skills?

Plan on two to four hours a month of protected learning time, plus an hour or two a week of practice on real work.

### When should a team start using AI agents?

After Level 4. Build written, approved workflows first. Agents come after that, not before.
