> **The short answer**
> Useful AI training for a marketing team starts with a skills assessment, then spends most of its time on the team's real work: writing prompts for tasks they do every month, practicing how to review AI output and agreeing on ground rules. People should leave with tested, saved prompts and a plan to follow up.

> **Three takeaways**
> - **Start with an assessment.** Confidential, about 15 minutes, so training goes where the gaps are.
> - **Practice on real work.** Each person writes and reviews prompts for a task they do every month.
> - **Leave with saved prompts.** Then follow up: teach-back, show-and-tell and a retake at the next review.

I've been building and running marketing workshops for a long time. My half-day master class on the customer journey taught me the first rule: four hours is a long time to hold an audience's attention. It takes great content, lots of interaction and a great workbook.

An AI marketing workshop needs all three, plus one more thing. People have to leave with something they'll use on Monday. Not a list of tools. A saved prompt, on their own work, that someone has already approved.

Here's what I think a useful AI marketing workshop looks like, and what to ask before you book one, with me or anyone else.

## Why doesn't most AI training stick?

**Because it focuses on tools and skips the people.** Teams don't stall because they lack tools.

![ATD 2026: employees averaged 16.7 hours of formal learning in 2025, about 1.4 hours a month.](/images/blog/ai-training-for-marketing-teams-1.png)
*A one-hour demo doesn't fix that. Training has to fit how the team already works.*

The Content Marketing Institute's 2027 research found that team skills and capabilities were the second biggest factor in effective B2B marketing programs (43%). Marketing technology came in eighth of 12 (15%). Yet only 9% of B2B marketers named human resources as a top investment area for 2027, compared with 30% for AI-powered tools (CMI, 2026).

And the learning time isn't there. In 2025, employees averaged 16.7 hours of formal learning for the whole year, about 1.4 hours a month (ATD, 2026).

So a one-hour demo doesn't fix it. A workshop has to fit into how the team already works, and keep going after it ends.

## What should happen before an AI workshop?

**A confidential skills assessment, so the training goes where the real gaps are.** Every training engagement I run starts with a needs analysis.

Before a workshop, each person takes a confidential readiness assessment. It takes about 15 minutes. They rate their own marketing skills, their AI skills and the organization as a whole. Leadership sees only the combined results, never one person's answers.

That tells us where the gaps really are. Then the results go straight into the curriculum. If the team is strong on content and weak on reviewing AI output, that's where the time goes.

Honest beats high. Low scores tell us where to help.

## What should an AI marketing workshop cover?

**Mostly practice, on real work: placing the team on the AI fluency ladder, writing prompts, reviewing AI output and agreeing on ground rules.** Here's how I structure it:

- **Place the team on the AI fluency ladder.** Level 1, not started. Level 2, experimenting. Level 3, using AI regularly. Level 4, integrated into your workflows. Most teams start at Level 2 or 3.
- **Practice CRAFT on real assignments.** Context, Role, Ask, Format, Tighten. Each person brings a task they do every month and writes a prompt for it, in the room.
- **Practice the review.** This is the skill most training skips. Knowing what good looks like, catching what's wrong, fixing the voice. AI drafts. People decide.
- **Agree on the ground rules.** Who approves what goes public. What data never goes in a prompt. What tools are approved.

## How do I choose an AI workshop or trainer?

**Ask five questions before you book any AI workshop.**

![Five questions to ask before booking AI training: assessment, own work, what we leave with, who reviews output, what happens after.](/images/blog/ai-training-for-marketing-teams-3.png)
*If the answer to number four is "AI is pretty accurate now," keep looking.*

**1. Does it start with an assessment?** If the trainer doesn't know where your team is, they'll teach to the middle. Ask how they'll measure your team before and after.

**2. Will we work on our own work?** Skills that aren't used within a week fade. Bring a real project to every session. A prompt built on your monthly report is worth ten built on a made-up example.

**3. What do we leave with?** You want saved, tested prompts in a place your whole team can find them, not just notes. A prompt you save and share is a process. A prompt you retype is a habit.

**4. Who reviews the output, and how?** Ask how the workshop teaches people to check AI's work. If the answer is "AI is pretty accurate now," keep looking.

**5. What happens after?** The best follow-up is simple. Have each person who attends teach the highlights back to the team. Run a monthly AI show-and-tell: one workflow that saved time, one that failed. Then retake the assessment at your next quarterly review and compare.

## FAQ

### How long should an AI training workshop be?

Long enough to practice on real work, which a one-hour demo isn't. A half day works if it has strong content, lots of interaction and a workbook, because four hours is a long time to hold attention.

### What should employees leave AI training with?

Tested prompts built on their own tasks, a shared prompt library, draft AI usage rules and a marketing compliance checklist. Not just notes.

### What AI skill does most training skip?

Reviewing the output. Knowing what good looks like, catching what's wrong and fixing the voice. AI drafts. People decide.

### How do you measure whether AI training worked?

Assess the team before the workshop, then have everyone retake the assessment at your next quarterly review and compare. A monthly show-and-tell also shows which workflows are saving time.
