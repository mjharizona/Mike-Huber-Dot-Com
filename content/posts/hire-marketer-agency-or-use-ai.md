When should you hire marketing help? I've been on every side of that question: the agency, the in-house team, the consultant and, lately, the person doing it himself with AI.

Here's what I've learned. "Should we hire someone?" is the wrong question. The better one is: who will own each piece of the work?

## What are my options for getting marketing done?

**There are four: train your team, hire, automate with AI or outsource.** When Loren and I wrote the first edition of our book, the choice was simple: your internal team or an outside agency. AI added a third option for many tasks, and the smartest teams now mix all four:

- **Train the team you have.** Best for skills you'll need permanently and that your people have the aptitude to learn.
- **Hire someone new.** Best when you need deep expertise every day, like a full-time content lead or paid media manager.
- **Automate with AI.** Best for repetitive tasks where AI is reliable and a trained team member reviews the output.
- **Outsource to an agency or freelancer.** Best for specialized or occasional work: technical SEO, video production, a website rebuild.

![Four ways to get marketing done: train your team, hire, automate with AI, outsource.](/images/blog/hire-marketer-agency-or-use-ai-1.png)
*Most businesses end up mixing all four.*

Most deliverables end up with more than one check. AI drafts, a team member refines, an agency advises.

## Can a small business do its own marketing with AI?

**For many tasks, yes, once the hard decisions and the setup are done.** I'll use my own family as the example.

My brother Dan owns [Wild West Custom Pools](https://www.wildwestcustompools.com) in the Austin area. Last year he needed a new position, a new website and an ad plan. I helped him with all of it, because he's my brother. AI did the research and the first drafts. Dan and I made the calls.

What happened next is the part I like best. Dan is running it himself now. He's added a pricing tool, city pages and a better lead system on his own.

That's the right shape for a lot of businesses. Get help with the hard decisions and the setup. Then own it.

## How much does it really cost to hire a marketer?

**More than the salary.** Budget local wages plus about 43% for benefits and payroll taxes, plus training.

Look up local wages in the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics (bls.gov/oes). Then add the extra for benefits and payroll taxes, plus training. In June 2026, benefits alone were 30% of private employers' compensation costs (BLS Employer Costs for Employee Compensation).

And remember that BLS doesn't track paid media, SEO, content or marketing automation specialists as separate jobs. For those, use a current salary survey or local job postings.

Building an internal team takes real time and money: hiring the right people, training them and giving them the right tools. Plan for it now, not in month four.

## What should I ask a marketing agency before I hire it?

**Ask how it uses AI, who reviews the work and how it protects your data.** An agency gives you skills and capacity without adding headcount. To get your money's worth, inspect what you expect. Review the work. Hold the agency to what it contracted to deliver. Talk often.

You want a partner that uses AI to deliver more value, not one that bills senior-strategist rates for lightly edited AI output. Four good questions:

1. Which tasks do you use AI for?
2. Who reviews AI-assisted work before we see it?
3. How do you protect our data?
4. How has AI changed your pricing?

## Should I hire a freelancer or an agency?

**Hire a freelancer for one skill you need now and then.** Hire an agency when the work needs several skills working together, or someone to cover it when a person is out. I've hired both, and I spent years on the agency side. Neither is better. They solve different problems.

![Freelancer versus agency: one skill and fast, or a team with coordination and a bench.](/images/blog/hire-marketer-agency-or-use-ai-2.png)
*Neither is better. They solve different problems.*

A freelancer is one person with one deep skill: a copywriter, a designer, a paid search specialist. You usually pay less, you talk to the person doing the work, and things move fast. The trade-off is capacity. If your freelancer gets sick, books up or takes a full-time job, the work stops. And if you hire three freelancers, someone on your side has to manage them and make the pieces fit. That someone is usually you.

An agency gives you a team: strategy, writing, design, media and reporting under one contract, with an account manager to keep it all moving. You pay for that coordination, and for the bench that covers vacations and turnover. The risk is that a senior person sells you and a junior person does the work, or AI does it with a light edit. Ask who will actually do the work, not just who's in the pitch.

Four questions settle it:

1. **How many skills does the work need?** One skill points to a freelancer. Several that have to fit together point to an agency.
2. **How often?** A project or occasional work suits a freelancer. Every week suits an agency, or a hire.
3. **Who on your team will manage it?** If no one has the time, an agency's account management is worth paying for.
4. **What happens if that person disappears?** If the work can't stop, like ads that are running or monthly reporting, you want a bench behind it.

Whichever you choose, put it in writing: what gets delivered, by when, who owns the files and logins, and how either side ends the arrangement. Your website, domain and ad accounts should be in your name, not theirs.

## How do I decide who should do each marketing task?

**List the deliverables, ask your team first, flag the risky ones and keep strategy inside.** Five steps you can start today:

**1. List the deliverables, not the roles.** Write down every piece of marketing work your plan needs: the website updates, the monthly email, the ads, the reviews, the reporting. A job title hides the work. A list shows it.

![Five steps: list deliverables, ask your team first, mark risky work, keep strategy inside, use a neutral voice.](/images/blog/hire-marketer-agency-or-use-ai-3.png)
*Your strategy must be owned by someone who will be there next year.*

**2. Ask your team first.** Before you hire or outsource a task, ask whether someone already on your team could handle it with good AI tools and training. And ask them, not just yourself. The people doing the work know best what AI can and can't take on. Sometimes the answer is yes, sometimes no, but always ask.

**3. Mark the risky ones.** Some tasks are risky to hand to AI: anything with legal exposure, customer data or your reputation on the line. Those need a skilled person, inside or outside.

**4. Never outsource your strategy.** Agencies and AI can inform it, challenge it and execute it. But your strategy must be owned by someone inside your organization who will be there next year.

**5. Use outside help where a neutral voice matters.** When you're setting priorities, cutting programs or redrawing roles, the boss shouldn't always run the meeting. A neutral facilitator keeps the discussion fair and lets everyone take part, including the boss.

## FAQ

### Is it cheaper to hire an agency or a full-time marketer?

It depends on how much work you have and how often. Price a hire fully, with local wages plus about 43% for benefits and payroll taxes, plus training. An agency gives you skills and capacity without adding headcount.

### When should a small business hire a full-time marketer?

When you need deep expertise every day, like a full-time content lead or paid media manager. For occasional or specialized work, an agency or freelancer usually fits better.

### Should I hire a freelancer or an agency?

A freelancer fits one skill you need now and then, like a copywriter or a designer, and usually costs less. An agency fits work that needs several skills working together every week, and it covers you when someone is out. Either way, keep your website, domain and ad accounts in your name.

### Can AI replace a marketing agency?

For repetitive tasks, AI can do much of the work if a trained team member reviews the output. Specialized or occasional work, like technical SEO or video production, still suits an agency. Anything with legal exposure, customer data or your reputation on the line needs a skilled person.

### Should I outsource my marketing strategy?

No. Agencies and AI can inform it, challenge it and execute it, but someone inside your business who will be there next year should own it.
