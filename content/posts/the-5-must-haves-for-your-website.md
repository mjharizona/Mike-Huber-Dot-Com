> **The short answer**
> Before you spend another dollar on traffic, make sure your website does five things: says clearly who you help and how, shows proof on every page, makes it easy to contact you, answers the questions buyers actually ask, and loads fast on a phone. Fix those first. Then add traffic.

> **Three takeaways**
> - **Test it as a buyer.** Fill out your own form from your phone this week.
> - **Put proof everywhere.** A testimonials page nobody visits doesn't count.
> - **Answer real questions.** That's how you show up in Google and in AI answers.

Every week I look at websites for business owners, and every week I see the same five gaps. They aren't design problems. They're the foundation that separates a website that works from one that just exists.

![The five must-haves: clear offer, proof, easy contact, real answers, fast on phones. Then add traffic.](/images/blog/website-five-must-haves.png)
*Fix the foundation first. Every dollar of traffic after that works harder.*

## 1. Can a visitor tell in five seconds who you help?

**Your home page has to answer three questions right away:** who you serve, what problem you solve and why they should trust you. Visitors decide within seconds whether they're in the right place.

A clever tagline isn't a value proposition. For my brother Dan's company, [Wild West Custom Pools](https://www.wildwestcustompools.com), we settled the message before anyone designed a page: "We only build 12 pools a year." It tells the right buyer exactly what kind of builder he is.

**Check it:** show your home page to someone who doesn't know your business for five seconds. Ask them what you do and who it's for.

## 2. Is there proof on every page?

**People don't trust companies. They trust other people.** Your site needs proof wherever a buyer is making up their mind, not just on one page:

- **Reviews** from real customers, with specific results
- **Project stories** that show the before and after
- **Logos** of customers or partners people recognize
- **Numbers.** "Trained more than 200 teams" beats "we have experience."

Put a review next to every call to action. That's the moment a buyer needs it most.

## 3. Can someone contact you in ten seconds?

**If a buyer can't figure out how to reach you in ten seconds, they leave.** Make it impossible to miss:

- A phone number in the header that people can tap to call
- A contact form that actually works. Test it every month.
- A clear promise on reply time: "We reply within one business day"
- More than one way to reach you: form, phone, email or a calendar link

Then keep the promise. Speed matters more than most owners think. Here's [how fast you should respond to a new lead](https://mikehuber.com/blog/how-fast-to-respond-to-leads).

## 4. Does your site answer the questions buyers ask?

**Google and AI assistants both look for the page that best answers the question.** Your content has to match what your buyers actually ask, not what you wish they'd ask.

Start with the questions your customers ask you on the phone. Then check Google Search Console to see what people searched before they found you. Write a page or a post for each important question, with the answer in the first two sentences. That's what AI assistants like ChatGPT quote, too.

> **Try this: find the questions you haven't answered**
> Paste this into ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini. Replace the brackets first.
>
> ```
> Context: I run [type of business] in [city]. My website pages are: [list your page titles].
> Role: Act as a buyer who is about to hire a business like mine.
> Ask: List the 15 questions you would want answered before you call. Then mark which ones my pages already answer and which they don't.
> Format: A table with three columns: question, answered (yes/no), the page that should answer it.
> Tighten: Use plain language. Don't invent anything about my business; put anything you need from me in [brackets].
> ```

## 5. Is it fast on a phone?

**Slow is broken.** Many of your visitors are on a phone, and they leave a slow page before it finishes loading. Google also uses page experience signals, including speed, when it ranks pages.

Run your home page through Google PageSpeed Insights. If the mobile score is low, the fixes are usually simple:

- Compress and resize your images
- Remove plugins and scripts you don't use
- Turn on caching
- Move to better hosting if you've outgrown it

## Where should you start?

**Start with the one that's costing you the most leads.** For most businesses, that's number 3: a form that doesn't arrive, or a reply that takes days. Then work down the list.

## FAQ

### What should be on a small business home page?

A clear line on who you help and how, proof such as reviews, an easy way to contact you, links to the answers buyers want most, and a page that loads fast on a phone.

### How do I know if my website is losing leads?

Fill out your own form from your phone, time how long a reply takes and check that every page has a next step. Here are [seven places small businesses lose leads](https://mikehuber.com/blog/why-small-business-losing-leads).

### Do I need a new website to fix these?

Usually not. Most of these are fixes to the site you have: a clearer headline, reviews in more places, a working form and smaller images.
