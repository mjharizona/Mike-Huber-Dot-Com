> Before you build anything with AI, ask four questions. Does it serve the goal you wrote down before you started? Who uses it, and does it pay in money, leads or time saved? Who keeps it alive, by name and on a schedule? What will you stop doing to make room? If any answer is "I don't know," wait.

The best marketing advice I gave anyone this year, I gave to my brother Dan. Then I spent the next six weeks ignoring it myself.

It took an old friend at lunch to point it out. He said what I needed to hear: just because you can doesn't mean you should.

Here's how I got there, and why I use that filter now.

## How does AI make it easy to take on too much?

**It makes every next step look small.** Each project took an hour or two, and each one made sense standing on the one below it.

![One yes at a time: a CV, a website, Dan's business, the book, then the PDF, website, tools, guides and slides.](/images/blog/decide-which-ai-projects-to-do-1.png)
*Every one made sense standing on the step below it.*

It started with an email asking for my current CV. One small job. AI helped me update it in less than an hour.

Then the CV didn't match my website, so I rebuilt that. Under two hours.

Then Dan called. He owns [Wild West Custom Pools](https://www.wildwestcustompools.com) in the Austin area, and we used AI to reposition his whole business.

Then I thought about the book Loren Ankarlo and I published in 2023. It barely touched on AI. What if I updated it? That was the job. Realistically, I'd just update the book.

But it was so easy to build the rest. The interactive PDF, rebuilt from the book's files. A website for the book. Online tools, because a PDF can't calculate and a website can. A field guide for every skill. A facilitator's guide for whoever teaches each one. Training slides with the script already in the notes.

Ten yeses. Every one of them good work. Every one made sense standing on the step below it. And at the top of the stairs, I had one more idea: with all this material, we could do some consulting.

That's when I had lunch with Arnie.

## What should you ask before you start another project?

**Two plain questions: how will this make money, and what's your main focus?** A friend asked me both, and I didn't have good answers.

Arnie is an old friend and one of the trusted advisors on this project. He'd gone through everything we'd built. I told him about all the things we were going to do.

He was overwhelmed. Then he asked, "How are you going to make money? What's your main focus?"

I told him. Sell the book. Sell subscriptions to the website. And consulting. Three businesses.

He said that was way too much. Then he asked, "Why would you do that?"

I said, "Well, it's a lot of fun. And we could make some money doing it."

And he said it: "Just because you can doesn't mean you should."

> “Just because you can doesn't mean you should.”

> — Arnie, an old friend and trusted advisor

## Why is "should we?" harder to answer with AI?

**Because AI took away the limit that used to decide for us.** When almost everything is possible, "should we?" is the only question left, and most of us have never had to practice it.

![Building got cheap; everything after building didn't: review, updates, upkeep and a later finish line.](/images/blog/decide-which-ai-projects-to-do-2.png)
*Every yes is also a promise to keep that thing alive.*

For most of my career, the hard question was "Can we do this?" Do we have the budget, the people, the time, the developer? That question did the deciding for us. If we couldn't afford it, we didn't build it.

AI took that question away. Now almost everything is a yes.

And here's what nobody shows you in the AI demo. Building got cheap. Everything after building didn't. Every piece AI drafts still needs a person to read it and say yes. Every guide and tool has to be updated when the book changes. More pieces means a later finish line. Every yes is also a promise to keep that thing alive.

## How do you keep AI projects from piling up?

**Decide what "done" means before you start, name who maintains each piece and get someone to push back.** Here are five lessons you can use Monday.

**1. AI is the orchestra. You're the conductor.** It'll play anything you put in front of it, as long and as loud as you let it. It never asks, "Are you sure?" The conductor's job is to pick the piece before anyone sits down, and decide what doesn't get played.

**2. Write down "done" before you start.** Finish this sentence: "This project is done when ____." Mine should have said, "...the second edition is on Amazon." Everything else would have had to argue its way in. You can make AI hold you to it. Before you start, ask it to write your "done" sentence with you, then tell you whenever a new request moves you toward that sentence or away from it.

**3. Before you build it, ask who keeps it alive.** A name, not "the team." How often? Monthly, quarterly, every edition? And what does it replace? If the answer is "nothing," it isn't progress. It's just more.

**4. Find your Arnie.** You need at least one person who isn't excited about your project. If you don't have one handy, AI can play the part, but only if you ask, because by default it's eager to help you build. The prompt is in the Try this box below. Then get a real person too. AI will give you good reasons. A friend who knows you will tell you the truth.

**5. Your "no" is your position.** The advice I gave Dan was to only build 12 pools a year. It works because it turns most buyers away. A marketing team that does twelve things well beats one that does forty things halfway. Who are you not for? What will you stop doing?

## What's a simple filter for AI projects?

**Four questions.** Run every AI project, big or small, through them:

1. Does it serve the sentence, the "done when" you wrote before you started?
2. Who uses it, and does it pay? In money, leads or time saved. Name it.
3. Who keeps it alive? A name and a schedule.
4. What will I stop doing to make room for it?

## FAQ

### Should I use AI for everything in my marketing?

No. AI can build almost anything, but every piece still needs a person to approve it and someone to keep it updated. Use it where it serves your goal and pays back in money, leads or time saved.

### How do I prioritize AI projects when I have too many ideas?

Score them. Weight the criteria that matter to your business, score each idea and let the math rank the list. Then start with the top few and leave the rest for later.

### What's the hidden cost of building things with AI?

Upkeep. Building got cheap, but every guide, tool or page still needs a person to review it and update it when things change. Every yes is a promise to keep that thing alive.

### Can AI tell me not to build something?

Yes, but only if you ask. By default it's eager to help you build. Ask it to act as a blunt advisor and give you the three strongest reasons not to, then check with a real person who knows you.
