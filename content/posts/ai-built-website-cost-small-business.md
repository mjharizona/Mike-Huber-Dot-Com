I rebuilt mikehuber.com in under two hours. My brother's pool company website took less than a workday for the first complete version. So what does an AI-assisted website cost?

Less than it used to, in build time. More than most people expect, in everything around the build. Let me show you where the time and money actually go.

## How fast can AI build a website?

**Hours, not weeks, for the first version.** I'm not a developer. On WordPress I could edit a page and add a plugin. Anything custom meant hiring someone.

![Peng et al. 2023: developers with an AI coding assistant finished in 71 minutes instead of 161, about 56% faster.](/images/blog/ai-built-website-cost-small-business-1.png)
*The build got fast. Everything around it didn't.*

When I first rebuilt my site with AI, it was plain HTML. I told my son, who's a principal engineer at Carvana, what I'd done. He asked about the tech stack, and when I told him, his answer was, "Hey Dad, join the 21st century." Build it in React, Node.js and JavaScript, he said. So I asked AI to rebuild it, and about ten minutes later I had an updated site on a modern stack.

The research points the same way. In a controlled study, developers using an AI coding assistant finished a task in 71 minutes instead of 161, about 56% faster (Peng et al., 2023).

So yes, the build got fast. But look at what that first draft of my brother Dan's site still needed.

## Where does the time go after AI builds the first draft?

**Into facts, real proof, forms and security.** Dan owns [Wild West Custom Pools](https://www.wildwestcustompools.com) in the Austin area. AI built the first complete site in less than a workday. Then came nine rounds of review and revision. Here's some of what those rounds covered:

- **Facts.** AI's first draft said Dan personally designs every pool. He doesn't; he works with a dedicated designer. A person who knows the business had to read every word.
- **Real proof.** Placeholders were swapped for real pool photos, eight real Google reviews and real pricing tiers. AI can't supply those. You have to.
- **The contact form.** It took about seven rounds of troubleshooting before every inquiry reliably reached Dan's inbox.
- **Security and privacy.** After launch, a review found server log files with visitors' personal information that were publicly accessible. They were deleted. When spam started, invisible spam protection was added.

## What does it cost to maintain a website built with AI?

**It's an ongoing cost, and it's the one most people forget.** The other cost nobody demos is upkeep. Links break. Facts go stale. Prices change. The tools themselves change every few months. Every page you add is something someone has to keep current.

That's why every site I build can come with a maintenance plan. It's also why I tell people to think about who will keep the site current before they decide how big it should be.

## What else does a website cost besides the build?

**The build is only part of the bill.** The website is the stage. Someone still has to supply what goes on it, and someone has to bring people to it. Ask what a quote includes, because these are often separate:

- **Positioning and messaging.** If you don't know what makes you different, the site can't say it. Dan's site worked because his position was settled before anyone wrote a headline. That took research, several options and real decisions. If your business needs that work, budget for it as its own project.
- **Photography and video.** Real photos of your work, your team and your place of business beat any stock image. A professional shoot is its own cost, and so is the time to plan it. Phone photos can work if they're sharp, well lit and real.
- **Graphics and branding.** A logo refresh, icons, diagrams, a consistent look. If your brand isn't settled, AI will guess, and you'll pay for it in revision rounds.
- **Interactive pieces.** Calculators, pricing and quote tools, booking, galleries, maps. Each one has to be designed, built, tested and kept working. Dan added a pricing tool after launch. That's a good addition, and it's one more thing to maintain.
- **Copy and proof.** Someone has to write, check and approve every word, gather reviews you have permission to use and keep prices current.
- **Domain, hosting, email and tools.** Small monthly and yearly bills that add up: hosting, the domain renewal, form and email services, a scheduling app.
- **Marketing after launch.** A new site doesn't bring visitors by itself. Search, your Google Business Profile, ads, email and social media all take time or money. Budget for getting found, not just for being online.

None of these is a reason not to build. They're the reason to plan the whole budget before you start, so the build doesn't use it all up.

## How can you keep website costs down?

**Be ready before the build starts, and plan for revisions.** Here are five ways, whoever builds it.

**1. Have your proof ready before you start.** Real photos of your work. Real reviews you have permission to use. Your prices or price ranges. Your answers to the questions buyers ask most. The fastest builds I've seen were fast because the owner had this ready on day one.

**2. Know your position before you write a word.** Dan's site worked because the message was settled first: "We only build twelve pools a year." If you don't know what makes you different, AI will write something any competitor could use, and you'll spend your revision rounds arguing about headlines.

**3. Budget for revisions, not just the build.** Nine rounds isn't a sign something went wrong. It's what it takes to get the facts, the voice and the proof right. Plan for it.

**4. Test every form and check every privacy setting.** Submit your own form from a phone and a computer. Ask who can see the data your site collects and where it's stored. AI builds fast, but someone must check security and privacy.

**5. Decide what AI is allowed to touch.** If AI is writing your copy, answering visitors or reading your customer data, write down the rules first: which tools are approved, what data stays out and who approves what goes live. ## Is an AI-built website cheaper in the end?

**To build, yes. An AI-assisted website costs less to build than it did a few years ago.** It still costs your time, your decisions and someone to keep it alive. Plan for those, and the build is the easy part.

## FAQ

### Can AI build a small business website by itself?

AI can build a complete first draft fast. Dan's took less than a workday. A person still has to check every fact, add real photos and reviews, test the forms and check privacy.

### How many revision rounds should I expect?

Plan for several. Dan's site went through nine rounds of review and revision, and that's what it took to get the facts, the voice and the proof right.

### What should I have ready before a website build?

Real photos of your work, reviews you have permission to use, your prices or price ranges and answers to the questions buyers ask most.

### What should I budget for besides the website build?

Positioning and messaging if your business needs it, photography, graphics, any interactive tools like calculators or booking, copy and reviews, hosting and other small subscriptions, and the marketing that brings visitors after launch. Ask what each quote includes.

### Do AI-built websites need maintenance?

Yes. Links break, facts go stale, prices change and the tools change every few months. Decide who will keep the site current before you decide how big it should be.
