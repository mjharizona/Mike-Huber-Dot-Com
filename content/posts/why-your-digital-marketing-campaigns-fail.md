I've worked with hundreds of organizations on their digital marketing. The campaigns that fail almost always have the same root cause: they started with tactics instead of strategy.

Here's what I mean — and more importantly, how to fix it.

## The Tactics-First Trap

"We need to be on TikTok." "We should run Facebook ads." "Let's start a podcast."

These are tactics. They might be the right tactics. But starting there is like deciding to drive south before picking your destination.

**Strategy answers: Who are we targeting? What do they need? Where do they spend their time? What does success look like?**

Tactics answer: How do we execute that strategy?

Campaigns fail when they're built backward — when the tactic drives the plan instead of the plan driving the tactic.

## The Most Common Failure Modes

### 1. No Clear ICP (Ideal Customer Profile)

If you're targeting "everyone," you're targeting no one. The more specifically you can define your best customer — industry, role, pain points, where they research, how they buy — the more precisely you can target and message.

### 2. Misaligned Measurement

I see organizations declare campaigns successful based on vanity metrics (impressions, followers, clicks) while their actual revenue sits flat. If your campaign goal is "increase brand awareness" and your executive team measures success in closed deals, you're set up for failure before day one.

Align your metrics to business outcomes: pipeline generated, cost per qualified lead, conversion rate by channel.

### 3. Skipping the Middle of the Funnel

Most digital marketing obsesses over TOFU (top of funnel) — blog posts, social, paid ads driving traffic — and BOFU (bottom of funnel) — demos, trials, sales calls. But the middle — the nurture, the consideration content, the proof — is where most deals are won or lost.

Build content for every stage of the buyer's journey.

### 4. Giving Up Too Early

Organic strategies — SEO, content marketing, earned media — take 6–12 months to show real results. Most organizations pull the plug at month three. 

Set realistic timelines with your stakeholders before campaigns launch. Silence expectations now, or face them later.

### 5. No Feedback Loop

If your sales team isn't talking to your marketing team about lead quality, and your marketing team isn't sharing campaign data with leadership, you have a system that can't learn. Build in regular reviews. Close the loop.

---

The fix is simpler than you'd expect: slow down, define success clearly, build your strategy around your customer's actual journey, and commit to the timeline the strategy requires.
