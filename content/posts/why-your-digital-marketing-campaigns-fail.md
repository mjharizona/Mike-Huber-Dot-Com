> **The short answer**
> Most campaigns fail because they start with a tactic ("let's run Facebook ads") instead of a strategy. Decide who you're trying to reach, what they need, where they look and what success means in dollars. Then pick the tactics, give them enough time to work and review the results with sales every month.

> **Three takeaways**
> - **Strategy first.** A channel is a tactic, not a plan.
> - **Measure business results.** Leads, sales and cost per lead, not likes.
> - **Give it time.** Search and content usually take months, not weeks.

I've worked with hundreds of organizations on their marketing. The campaigns that fail almost always share the same root cause: they started with tactics instead of strategy.

![Strategy first, then tactics: who, what, where, success, then tactics, with a monthly review.](/images/blog/strategy-before-tactics.png)
*Answer the four strategy questions before you choose a single channel.*

## What's the difference between strategy and tactics?

**Strategy decides who, what, where and what success looks like. Tactics decide how.** "We need to be on TikTok." "We should run Facebook ads." "Let's start a podcast." Those are tactics. They might be the right ones. But starting there is like deciding to drive south before you pick your destination.

Strategy answers four questions. Who are we trying to reach? What do they need? Where do they spend their time? What does success look like? Tactics answer one: how do we carry that out?

Campaigns fail when they're built backward, when the tactic drives the plan instead of the plan driving the tactic.

## Why does targeting "everyone" fail?

**If you're targeting everyone, you're targeting no one.** The more clearly you can describe your best customer (their industry or situation, their problem, where they research and how they buy), the more precisely you can aim your message and your budget.

When we repositioned my brother Dan's company, [Wild West Custom Pools](https://www.wildwestcustompools.com), the biggest change wasn't a new ad. It was deciding who he was for: homeowners who could write a check for a luxury pool, not buyers shopping for the lowest price. Every ad, page and email got easier after that.

## Are you measuring the wrong things?

**Measure what the business cares about, not what's easy to count.** I see organizations call a campaign a success based on impressions, followers and clicks while sales stay flat. If the campaign goal is "awareness" and the owner measures success in closed deals, it's set up to fail before day one.

Agree on the measures before you launch: leads, cost per lead, sales from the campaign and conversion rate by channel. Write them down where everyone can see them.

## What happens in the middle of the funnel?

**The middle is where most sales are won or lost.** Most marketing pays attention to the top (posts, ads and traffic) and the bottom (demos, quotes and sales calls). The middle (follow-up emails, case studies, comparisons, proof) is what moves someone from "interested" to "ready."

Build something for every stage of the buyer's journey. A lead who isn't ready this month should hear from you again, with something useful, until they are.

## How long should you give a campaign?

**Longer than most owners do.** Search, content and earned media usually take six to twelve months to show real results. Many organizations pull the plug around month three.

Set the timeline with your team or your boss before the campaign launches. Agree on what you'll see at 30, 90 and 180 days, so no one expects month-twelve results in month two.

## Is anyone learning from the results?

**A campaign without a feedback loop can't get better.** If sales isn't telling marketing which leads were good, and marketing isn't sharing results with leadership, you have a system that can't learn.

Hold a short monthly review: what came in, what turned into business, what to keep, what to change and what to stop. Then adjust. Rinse and repeat.

## FAQ

### Why do most marketing campaigns fail?

Because they start with a tactic instead of a strategy. Without a clear customer, clear measures and enough time, even good creative won't produce results.

### How do I measure if a campaign is working?

Agree before launch on leads, cost per lead, sales from the campaign and conversion rate by channel. Review them every month with whoever handles sales.

### How long should a marketing campaign run before I judge it?

Paid ads can show results in weeks. Search, content and earned media usually need six to twelve months. Set the timeline before you launch.

## Your next three actions

1. Write down who your best customer is in two sentences.
2. Pick three measures that matter to the business, and share them with your team.
3. Put a 30-minute monthly results review on the calendar with sales.
