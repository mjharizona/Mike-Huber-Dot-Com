> **The short answer**
> Most small businesses lose leads after the lead arrives, not before. Check seven places: forms that don't reach your inbox, slow replies, pages with no clear next step, too few recent reviews, missing from AI answers, no follow-up for buyers who aren't ready, and leads you can't trace to a source. Fix those before you buy more traffic.

> **Three takeaways**
> - **Leads leak after they arrive.** Fix the leak before you add water.
> - **Check seven places.** Forms, reply speed, next steps, reviews, AI answers, follow-up and tracking.
> - **Fix the costliest first.** No more than three at a time.

There's a line in our book I come back to more than any other: fix the leak before you add water.

Most businesses I've worked with don't have a traffic problem first. They have a leak. Their marketing leaks leads in places nobody is watching, and then they spend more on ads to make up for it. If leads already come in and go nowhere, more traffic just means more leads going nowhere.

Here are seven places I see marketing leak leads, and how to check each one without hiring anyone.

## 1. Do your contact forms actually reach you?

**Don't assume they do. Test them.** When we launched my brother Dan's new pool company website, the most important conversion fix had nothing to do with headlines. The contact form took about seven rounds of troubleshooting, each followed by a test inquiry, before every inquiry reliably reached his inbox.

![Seven places small businesses lose leads: forms, reply speed, next steps, reviews, AI answers, follow-up, lead tracking.](/images/blog/why-small-business-losing-leads-1.png)
*Most businesses don't have a traffic problem first. They have a leak.*

None of that shows up in a conversion report. A form that loses one inquiry in five looks like a page that doesn't convert. For a business where one project starts at $90,000, a single lost inquiry is a five- or six-figure loss.

**Check it:** Fill out every form, click every button and call every phone number on your site, on a phone and on a computer. Confirm each inquiry actually arrives, at the right inbox.

## 2. How fast do you reply to a new lead?

**Aim for within one hour during business hours.** When someone asks to talk, speed wins, and most businesses are slow. In a study published in March 2026, Workato submitted demo requests to 114 B2B companies and timed the replies. Only one sent a personal email within five minutes. The average email reply took nearly 12 hours. Nearly one in five never replied by email at all (Workato, 2026). Workato sells automation software, and these were B2B companies, but the lesson applies to local businesses too.

**Check it:** Submit your own form at 10 AM on a Tuesday. Time how long it takes for a person to answer.

For the standards to set and how to hit them without new staff, read [how fast you should respond to a new lead](https://mikehuber.com/blog/how-fast-to-respond-to-leads).

## 3. Does every page tell visitors what to do next?

**It should, before they scroll.** A visitor should know what you offer, why it's right for them and what to do next. One page, one main action. And the button should say what happens next. "Submit" and "Click here" tell people nothing. "Book your free consultation" does.

**Check it:** Open your most important service page on your phone. Can you tell in five seconds what to do next? Ask someone who's never seen it the same question.

## 4. Are your reviews costing you customers?

**They can, especially if you have no recent ones.** Buyers check reviews before they check your website. In BrightLocal's 2026 Local Consumer Review Survey (1,002 U.S. consumers), 97% said they read reviews for local businesses, and 68% need at least four stars before they'll use one. In BrightLocal's 2026 Consumer Search Behavior study, of the consumers who rejected a business, 65% did so for a review-related reason: negative reviews, a low rating or no recent reviews.

![BrightLocal 2026: 97% read reviews for local businesses, 68% need four stars, 65% of rejections were review-related.](/images/blog/why-small-business-losing-leads-2.png)
*No recent reviews is a leak, and it's the easiest one to fix.*

That last one matters. No recent reviews is a leak, and it's the easiest one to fix.

**Check it:** Look at your Google Business Profile as a buyer would. When was your last review? Did you answer it? Then set up a simple habit of asking every happy customer.

## 5. Do AI assistants recommend your business?

**Ask them and find out.** Buyers now ask AI tools for recommendations. In the same BrightLocal review survey, 45% of consumers said they use ChatGPT or other AI tools to get recommendations for local businesses, up from 6% a year earlier. AI is still a new door, not the main one, but it's growing fast.

![BrightLocal 2026: 45% of consumers use ChatGPT or other AI tools for local recommendations, up from 6% a year earlier.](/images/blog/why-small-business-losing-leads-3.png)

**Check it:** Ask two or three AI assistants the questions your buyers ask, like "best [service] in [your city]." Are you mentioned? ## 6. What happens to leads who aren't ready to buy?

**Without follow-up, they buy from somebody else later.** Not every lead is ready to buy. Someone who asks for a quote is raising a hand. Someone who downloads a guide is doing research. If the only follow-up you have is a sales call, you lose the researcher. And if you call every person who downloads a guide, you annoy them.

The buyer who isn't ready this season may be ready next spring. Without a simple nurture sequence, they'll be ready for somebody else.

**Check it:** Write down what a buyer receives from you between their first contact and their decision to buy. If the answer is "nothing," that's your leak.

## 7. Do you know where each lead came from?

**If you don't, you can't do more of what worked.** Every lead should land in your CRM with its original source. And ask every new inquiry a simple question: "How did you find out about us?" Add an AI assistant as one of the options.

**Check it:** Pull your last ten leads. Can you say where each one came from? If not, fix the tracking before you change anything else.

## FAQ

### Why does my website get traffic but no leads?

Start with the basics: test that your forms arrive, and check that each page shows one clear next step. A form that quietly drops inquiries looks like a page that doesn't convert.

### Should I spend more on ads if I'm not getting enough leads?

Not until you fix the leaks. If leads already come in and go nowhere, more traffic just means more leads going nowhere.

### How quickly should I respond to a new lead?

Aim for within one hour during business hours. Submit your own form and time how long it takes a person to answer. Here's [how to set and hit a speed-to-lead standard](https://mikehuber.com/blog/how-fast-to-respond-to-leads).

### How do I track where my leads come from?

Log every lead in your CRM with its original source, and ask every new inquiry how they found you. Include an AI assistant as one of the options.
